"Strong opinions, held loosely" gets quoted like a complete instruction. The phrase is only half a method, dressed in the confidence of a whole one. What's missing is the half that actually costs something.

Holding an opinion loosely is supposed to mean staying open to being wrong, but when unwritten, it usually means the opposite. An opinion you've never had to state precisely drifts without anyone noticing, including yourself. Social pressure softens it, but the moment that pressure lifts, it sharpens back up. Then your strong opinion gets reported, sincerely, as having been consistent the whole time. Vagueness feels like humility because both are modest from the inside.

Humility means holding a specific, falsifiable claim. Change your opinion when a fact contradicts it. Vagueness never does that, because it was never specific enough to hold up to a fact in the first place.

The writing is the part that makes the looseness real instead of decorative. A written opinion has a date and words that can't retroactively mean something else. It leaves behind a future version of you who can be shown the gap between what you claimed and what turned out to be true. That confrontation is the entire mechanism. Without it, "held loosely" is just a description of how you'd like to think of yourself, offered with no way to check.

I made the same claim under a different name in an earlier piece about calibration. A forecast without a written-down number is a vibe wearing a forecast's clothes: it gets to be right in hindsight no matter what happens, because it was never pinned down enough to be wrong. The point generalizes past probability estimates and applies to any opinion you're claiming to hold loosely. If it isn't written somewhere with enough specificity that a later fact could embarrass you, the looseness is unverifiable, which functionally means it doesn't exist yet.

Strong opinions. Held loosely. Written down before you need credit for having held them loosely: that's the actual claim. Writing it down does more than record the opinion. Doing so supplies the mechanism that keeps "loosely" from meaning whatever belief you held only when it turned out to be convenient.

This site holds opinions specific enough to be wrong. Dated. Left up whether they held or not.
