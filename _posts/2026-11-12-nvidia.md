On November 12, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, became interim CEO. She keeps the CFO job. The board named Harvey Jones its first-ever chair and said it will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent successor. ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm))

Huang spent 33 years building Nvidia. The board spent those years overseeing it. Those are different jobs. Huang's record is settled. The board's is now open, and the first entries are not good.

## Four days

The resignation followed reports that Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence effort through a personal relationship, and a New York Times report of a separate affair with an executive who reports to him. China calls the spy allegation a smear. Nvidia gave no reason for his departure. ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6214845-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/why-did-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resign-and-who-is-colette-kress)) None of the allegations has been tested. This argument doesn't depend on any of them being true.

It depends on the timeline. On November 8, the board announced an independent investigation and said it had confidence in Huang and his leadership team. ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/Statement-from-NVIDIA-Board-of-Directors/default.aspx)) Nvidia told Business Insider that Huang was "committed to continuing to lead Nvidia as CEO without interruption." Four days later he was gone. The board met the night before to discuss removing him. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/ai-titan-jensen-huang-out-as-nvidia-ceo))

A board that declares confidence at the start of an investigation is placing a bet before it has the evidence. This one lost the bet within a week. That doesn't prove the directors were negligent. It does show they spoke before they knew what they were dealing with.

## The exit nobody scheduled

Succession planning usually gets discussed as a retirement problem. Retirement is the easy case. The founder picks a date, the board picks a successor, and everyone has a year to rehearse. A plan earns its cost in the other case, when the exit arrives without warning.

Nvidia just had that exit. The board's answer was an interim CEO who still runs finance. Kress is a sensible choice. She knows the business, and investors know her. But a CFO serving as CEO is a holding pattern.

The arrangement has a cost that shows up on paper. Federal securities law requires a public company's principal executive officer and principal financial officer to each certify its quarterly reports. At a company of Nvidia's size, those are normally two signatures from two people. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on November 17. Until the board finds a permanent CEO, the person vouching for the numbers and the person whose leadership the numbers judge are the same person. That can work for a few weeks. It is not what a board chooses when it has someone else ready.

The new chair matters more than it looks. Nvidia had never had one. Separating the chair from the CEO is the most common governance reform there is. Nvidia's board decided it needed a chair on the day Huang left. It did not reach that decision in the 33 years before.

## Who makes the call

Jones said he expects "the company's operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption." The roadmap is the easy part. Chips already in the pipeline will ship. The hard part comes when the plan has to change and someone has to decide how.

For three decades that someone was Huang. The arrangement produced extraordinary results. It also meant the executives around him rarely had to own the final call on anything that mattered. A board can name an interim CEO in one meeting. It cannot transfer 33 years of relationships and judgment in one meeting. What it can do is spend years making sure other executives hold real authority while the founder is still around to catch their mistakes.

## Indispensable is a risk

Tech has a habit of treating a founder's indispensability as proof of greatness. Sometimes it is. After 33 years, it is also a measure of what the board tolerated.

Nobody on Nvidia's board needed this week to learn that the company was built around one man. Bloomberg reported in January that Nvidia had no clear successor. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-27/nvidia-lacks-clear-successor-for-superstar-ceo-who-built-company)) The risk was visible from outside the boardroom. The directors left it in place, and on Thursday it came due.

Nvidia's own proxy statement warned that failing to plan for succession could hinder its strategy and execution. ([Dow Jones via Tiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2682733811)) On Thursday the board named someone to hold Huang's title. It has not shown who holds his authority.

If a major customer wants Nvidia to move a chip launch, who can tell them no and make it stick? The board had 33 years to make sure everyone at Nvidia knew the answer.
